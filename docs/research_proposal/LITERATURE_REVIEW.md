# Literature review: reliable individualisation (initial pass)

Status: 11 November 2026. This is a first-stage review, not an exhaustive novelty certification. Peer-reviewed sources are marked **P**; preprints **R**. Claims below are limited to the linked source material; where only an abstract or accessible excerpt was inspected, that is explicit.

## What the literature already covers

**Choosing whether to personalise.** Qin et al. [P, IEEE TSG 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2024.3448618) explicitly quantify fine-tuning gain and choose a global or personalised load forecaster at each time step. This is the closest title-and-problem threat. The accessible institutional description establishes the mechanism at a high level, but not whether its gain estimate transfers to entirely new individuals or datasets under a prospective cold-start protocol. A generic learned benefit gate is therefore not a defensible novelty claim.

**Combining global and local expertise.** Zhao, Cao and Wan [P, AAAI 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34448) combine static experts learned from offline intervals with a dynamic online expert and analyse convergence, regret and generalisation under their nonstationary interval setup. [Local prediction pools](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/for.3030) shrink local forecast weights toward equal weights when local evidence is sparse. These are direct baselines for any proposed cross-individual mixture. Their objectives are not, as presented, a calibrated estimate of *future individual adaptation benefit* for a new entity, but that distinction matters only if predicting the benefit improves decisions beyond their forecasts.

**Lightweight adaptation and reliability.** Liang et al. [P, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/6f7d90b1198fec96defd80b5ebd5bc81-Abstract-Conference.html) introduce bounded input/output delta-Adapters with local descent and drift bounds plus quantile/conformal correction. Huang, Ma and Michailidis [P, UAI 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v337/huang26b.html) use online martingale risk certificates to regularise a gated residual head under temporal dependence and shift. Thus, neither a bounded residual nor a gate that can close under shift is new by itself. A novel guarantee must specify an object they do not already control.

**Sequential evidence.** Arnold et al. [P, JRSS B 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkag066) construct anytime-valid sequential model confidence sets using e-processes under conditions on conditional loss differences. [Gibbs and Candès, JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/22-1218.html) address online conformal inference under shift, while [Bao et al., JMLR 2025](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/24-0452.html) address coverage after adaptive selection. A confidence-sequence trigger or online calibration layer alone is unlikely to be a new method. Arbitrary-shift, per-person no-harm guarantees are not implied by these papers and should not be promised.

**Patient-specific learning.** [Potosnak et al., 2025](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v287/main/assets/potosnak25a/potosnak25a.pdf) combine global glucose forecasting with patient-specific pharmacokinetics; [a 2025 glucose meta-learning study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40835650/) targets few-shot personalised prediction. [Hierarchical Bayesian glucose transfer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11293722/) already provides a partial-pooling route. These cover important pieces of the proposed setting. The distinctive question, if any, is whether cross-person evidence estimates *which adaptation action will help later*, beyond a strong hierarchical model's posterior and standard online expert weighting.

## Interpretation and open verification

The broad topic is established. A possible unresolved gap is **cross-entity learning of action-specific future benefit under scarce personal history, with prospective loss-based evaluation and a defensible risk comparator**. This is an inference from the inspected sources, not a verified absence in all literature. Before any novelty claim, inspect full methods and experiments of Qin et al. and the other closest papers, search their citations and subsequent work, and check whether a hierarchical contextual expert algorithm already yields the same objective and guarantees.
